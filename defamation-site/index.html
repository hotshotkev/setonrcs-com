Missouri defamation law

Missouri defamation disputes are governed by statutes, court decisions, and constitutional protections. The rules below are a high-level overview only; applying them requires reviewing the specific words, context, audience, and evidence.

Not legal advice or a legal notice. This page does not describe or accuse any person, resolve whether a statement is true or false, or demand that anyone take action. Consult a licensed Missouri attorney about a specific situation.

Speech and truth

Article I, Section 8 of the Missouri Constitution protects freedom of speech while also stating that people are responsible for abuses of that liberty. It expressly allows truth to be offered as evidence in libel and slander suits. The constitutional text does not decide how any particular dispute should come out.

Questions that may matter

Depending on the claim and context, legal analysis may consider whether a communication asserted a verifiable fact or expressed opinion, whether it was shared with someone other than the subject, whether it identified an individual, whether it was false, the applicable fault standard, any privilege or defense, and whether legally recognized harm can be shown. These are not a checklist that determines an outcome; standards can differ by context.

If you have a specific concern

  • Preserve the complete communication, its date, source, audience, and surrounding context.
  • Avoid editing, reposting, or escalating disputed statements while seeking advice.
  • Ask a Missouri attorney to review current law, possible defenses, and appropriate next steps before sending a legal demand.
